Many students think that reading English means looking up every new word. In fact, the harder problem is often the shape of the text. If you cannot see how the writer builds an argument, every sentence feels equally important.

Take the word address as an example. In one sentence it may mean to deal with a problem. In another it may mean a speech. Students who remember only one meaning often misread a whole paragraph.

A good reader always asks what the writer is trying to prove. That question keeps you from drowning in details. Read the map before you walk through the forest.
